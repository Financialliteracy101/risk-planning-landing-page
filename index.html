The Question Worth Asking

Having financial cover isn't the same as having a protection plan.

You may already have life cover through your employer or a personal policy. You may have investments, property and other assets.

But have you considered how all of these would work together if something happened to you?

If you were no longer able to provide, would your child still have the financial stability you've worked so hard to create?

Would their education remain affordable?

Could your family maintain its current lifestyle?

Would your income be replaced if illness or disability prevented you from working?

And have you considered how your assets and wishes should be provided for when minor children are involved?

Your Family's Protection Strategy

Three areas I consider when helping you protect your child's future.

The aim isn't simply to add more financial products. It's to consider the different areas of your family's financial life and how they work together.

01 — Protect Their Lifestyle

Protect the income your family depends on.

If illness or disability prevents you from earning, your family's financial needs don't stop. We consider how your income could be protected so your child isn't forced to experience unnecessary financial disruption.

02 — Protect Their Future

Provide for the future you've planned for them.

If you are no longer there to provide, appropriate life cover can help provide capital for the financial commitments and future needs you've been working towards for your child.

03 — Protect What You've Built

Make sure your wishes and assets are properly considered.

Estate planning forms an important part of the bigger picture — particularly when minor children are involved. Your assets, beneficiaries and wishes need to be considered as part of your overall protection strategy.
