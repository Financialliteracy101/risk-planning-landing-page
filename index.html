The Financial Protection Gap

Having policies isn't the same as having a protection strategy.

Many successful professionals have accumulated life cover, retirement funds, investments and other financial products over the years.

But these pieces are often purchased at different times, from different providers and without considering how they work together.

If you weren't here to provide for your child, would everything you've built still work?

Would there be enough capital to replace your income?

Would your child's education and lifestyle remain affordable?

Would your assets be transferred efficiently?

Would the people you trust actually know what to do?

And perhaps most importantly — would your child have the financial stability you intended for them?

A Complete Protection Strategy

Protect more than just your life.

Your protection strategy should consider the income that supports your family, the capital your child may need and how your assets should be managed and transferred.

01 — Income Protection

Protect the income your family depends on.

02 — Life Cover

Create financial security for the people you leave behind.

Life cover should be considered in the context of your family's actual financial needs — not simply as an arbitrary amount of cover.

03 — Estate Planning

Make sure what you've built reaches the people you intended.

Your protection strategy should also consider how your assets are structured, managed and transferred to your beneficiaries.
